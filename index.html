Area 01 — The foundation

One autonomy platform beneath every airframe

What it is

Treat autonomy as a platform rather than a per-programme build: perception, navigation, coordination and mission logic developed once as a shared engine, with adapters per airframe. Sheshnaag, Abhimanyu, HAPS and the civil vehicles draw on one foundation, and a new airframe plugs into it rather than starting from the ground up.

Why it matters

Three returns that compound. Each new airframe costs a fraction of the last, because the hard part exists. Vehicles on a joint mission coordinate through one shared layer, so adding a vehicle is configuration rather than a project. And flight data from any airframe trains the engine all of them run on — hardening proven once holds everywhere.

What I'd bring

This is the shape of problem I run. At Avathon, one platform carries five concurrent programmes across ten-plus enterprise accounts — asset performance, visual AI, inventory, supply chain, trade compliance — and they compound because a shared data and knowledge layer sits beneath all of them: what the visual AI sees on the floor informs what maintenance predicts, which informs what supply chain routes. The call I make daily is the one this turns on: what belongs in the shared foundation, and what belongs to the individual mission.

Area 02 — The proof

From flight test to proven in the field

What it is

The discipline that carries a platform from first flight to fielded and operationally trusted: instrumented deployments, a data loop turning every mission into training input, autonomy that ships updates rather than freezing at handover, and hardening against active jamming proven in simulation before it is met for real.

Why it matters

Operational proof is the currency defence customers buy in. Shield AI's international credibility came from V-BAT flying real sorties under live electronic warfare and feeding that back into its autonomy — a pipeline decision made early, not a research breakthrough. Sheshnaag has flown. Everything after first flight is a delivery timeline, and the data only compounds if you instrument before you deploy.

What I'd bring

Everything between a first flight and a fielded system is delivery: a test campaign sequenced against engineering capacity, deployments instrumented so the data comes back usable, and a release rhythm that lets autonomy keep improving after handover rather than freezing at it. That is the structure I build — the plan, the cadence, the readiness gates, and the reporting that keeps engineering, leadership and the customer seeing the same picture. I have run it as both Technical Manager and Programme Manager, so the trade-off calls are mine to make rather than mine to relay.

Area 03 — The counterweight

A commercial line that balances the research

What it is

A non-defence line that earns on its own terms — air taxis and air ambulances, air couriers and medical logistics, disaster response, infrastructure inspection, stratospheric telecom relay. In an organisation that is roughly 70% R&D, this is the counterweight: work that funds the research rather than waiting on it, sold to customers who buy on commercial timelines.

Why it matters

Commercial airspace is won on certification, not on capability. Carrying people or cargo over populated ground means type certification, DGCA and international airworthiness approval, BVLOS clearance, documented safety cases, failure-mode analysis and a test campaign that stands up to a regulator. That is the real barrier between NRT's autonomy and these markets — and it is a documentation and test-discipline problem, not a research one.

What I'd bring

Standing up a commercial line is a strategy problem and a delivery problem at once. Which missions to enter first, what the offer is — airframe, autonomy licensed into another manufacturer's platform, or a service — and how the money works: SOW structure, milestone terms, Opex and deferred-Opex treatment, revenue recognition. Then certification has to actually land, which means a test campaign sequenced against capacity and evidence produced in the order a regulator reads it. I have carried both sides across ten-plus enterprise accounts with full retention, and I know these buyers — Avathon's customers are this market.
